**Background.** Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most-diagnosed cancer worldwide. The gut microbiome — and in particular oral-derived pathobionts such as *Fusobacterium nucleatum* — has been mechanistically implicated in tumour initiation and progression via FadA-mediated E-cadherin/beta-catenin signalling and tumour-permissive immune modulation. We asked whether a stool metagenomic signature could reliably identify CRC across populations, and at which point along the adenoma-carcinoma sequence the cancer-defining signal emerges.

**Methods.** We re-analysed 1,522 stool shotgun metagenomes from ten case-control cohorts on three continents (674 CRC, 665 controls, 183 adenomas; curatedMetagenomicData). Random Forest classifiers trained on MetaPhlAn species profiles were evaluated under country-aware leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation, with same-country cohorts excluded from training to remove population structure. TreeSHAP identified driver species; the same design was applied to healthy-vs-adenoma and adenoma-vs-CRC tasks.

**Results.** The classifier discriminated CRC from controls with pooled AUC **0.781 (95% CI 0.757-0.805, n = 1,339)** and per-cohort AUC >= 0.69 in every cohort. Four oral pathobionts dominated TreeSHAP rankings: *Gemella morbillorum*, *Parvimonas micra*, *Peptostreptococcus stomatis*, and *Fusobacterium nucleatum* — reproducible across Random Forest and XGBoost. Healthy-vs-adenoma classification was near chance (AUC 0.561); adenoma-vs-CRC was moderate (0.671), driven by the same oral-pathobiont signature. Stool metagenomic discrimination is therefore weak at the adenoma stage and emerges at malignant transformation.

**Conclusions.** *F. nucleatum*, *P. micra*, *P. stomatis*, and *G. morbillorum* form a reproducible stool-metagenomic CRC biomarker panel candidate. Their late emergence positions stool metagenomics as a complement to colonoscopy and FIT for advanced lesions and post-resection surveillance, rather than an early-adenoma screen.
