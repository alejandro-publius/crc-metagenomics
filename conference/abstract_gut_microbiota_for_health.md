**Background.** Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most-diagnosed cancer worldwide. Current screening relies on colonoscopy (invasive, expensive) and faecal immunochemical testing (modest sensitivity for early lesions). Stool metagenomics is a promising non-invasive complement, but cross-cohort classifier performance and the biological identity of the discriminating signal must be established before clinical translation.

**Methods.** We assembled 1,522 stool shotgun metagenomes from ten case-control cohorts (curatedMetagenomicData; 674 CRC, 665 controls, 183 adenomas) spanning seven countries on three continents. We trained Random Forest classifiers on MetaPhlAn species profiles under a strict cross-cohort leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) design that additionally excludes same-country cohorts from training to remove population-level confounding. We then applied the design to adenoma-vs-control and adenoma-vs-CRC tasks across the four adenoma-containing cohorts (n = 183 adenomas), and used TreeSHAP to identify the species driving classifier decisions.

**Results.** The stool species classifier discriminated CRC from controls with pooled LODO AUC **0.781 (95% CI 0.757-0.805, n = 1,339)** and per-cohort AUC >= 0.69 in every cohort. Healthy-vs-adenoma classification was near chance (AUC 0.561), but adenoma-vs-CRC was moderate (0.671), supporting a stepwise model in which the diagnostic microbiome signature emerges at malignant transformation rather than at the adenoma stage. The signal is driven by four oral pathobionts: *Fusobacterium nucleatum*, *Peptostreptococcus stomatis*, *Parvimonas micra*, and *Gemella morbillorum* — reproducible across cohorts and consistent with the known mechanistic role of *F. nucleatum* in CRC.

**Conclusions.** Stool metagenomics may complement existing CRC screening for advanced lesions and post-resection monitoring; sensitivity for early adenoma is unlikely to be sufficient as a standalone screen.
