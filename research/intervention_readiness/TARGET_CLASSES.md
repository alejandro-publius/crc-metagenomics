# Two target classes

The atlas separates two jobs that are often blurred together.

## Effector targets

An effector target is a microbial gene or pathway whose activity is itself a
credible contributor to harm—for example, machinery that produces a genotoxin.
Silencing the effector aims to change the harmful function. Causal and
editability evidence are mandatory.

## Precision-address candidates

A precision address is a sequence that may distinguish a CRC-associated
microbial strain or clade from organisms that should be left alone. Its own
protein does not have to cause cancer. Instead, it could eventually help an
editing system recognize or selectively remove the relevant cells.

An associated gene family is only an **address candidate** until all of the
following are shown:

1. the signal survives cross-population held-out testing;
2. its taxonomic source is resolved rather than guessed from one archived
   representative sequence;
3. it is linked to a strain or clade with credible harmful-function evidence;
4. a targetable DNA region is conserved within that clade;
5. that region is absent from protected commensals and human sequence;
6. an appropriate delivery/editing platform can reach the organism;
7. gene-level recurrence is independently confirmed.

This distinction lets uncharacterized sequences remain useful research leads
without misrepresenting them as causal genes.
