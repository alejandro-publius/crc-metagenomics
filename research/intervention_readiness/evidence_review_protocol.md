# Structured causal and editability evidence review

**Rubric freeze:** 2026-08-08, before structured row-by-row extraction. The
reviewers were already aware of anchor papers; this is not a blinded systematic
review.

## Purpose

This review keeps three questions separate:

1. Does the microbial function contribute to a CRC-relevant phenotype?
2. Has the microbial gene or pathway been genetically perturbed?
3. Has a sequence-specific intervention been delivered in a disease-relevant
   microbial community?

Association AUC does not answer questions 1–3, and an engineered knockout does
not establish safe delivery.

## Causal evidence tiers

| Tier | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| C0 | Human or animal association without a function-specific perturbation |
| C1 | Biochemical, cell, or organoid perturbation linking the function to a CRC-relevant phenotype |
| C2 | Animal function-specific perturbation, preferably with an isogenic microbial mutant |
| C3 | Human molecular evidence directly attributable to the function, or a human intervention that perturbs it |

The highest tier is not a summary quality score. A C3 molecular signature does
not replace animal perturbation, and C2 animal evidence does not establish a
human treatment effect.

## Editability evidence tiers

| Tier | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| E0 | No microbial genetic perturbation demonstrated |
| E1 | Isogenic deletion, inactivation, or engineered strain tested ex vivo or after deliberate colonization |
| E2 | Sequence-specific payload delivered to the intended organism in a mixed community or living host |
| E3 | Delivered sequence-specific perturbation changes a CRC-relevant phenotype in vivo |

Publication status is recorded independently. A preprint can establish that an
experiment was reported, but it is labeled as non-peer-reviewed evidence.

## Extraction rules

- Primary research is required for tier assignment; reviews may orient searches
  but do not determine the maximum tier.
- Every row records the organism, function, model, perturbation, phenotype,
  publication status, and a short limitation.
- Genetic deletion after construction of a laboratory strain is E1, not proof
  of deliverability to a native microbiome.
- A pathway-level mutant in another organism supports the pathway mechanism but
  not species-specific delivery to the registry organism.
- No entry can be labeled experiment-ready while conservation, specificity,
  and delivery safety remain unresolved.
