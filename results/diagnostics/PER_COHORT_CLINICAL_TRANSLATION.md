# Per-cohort clinical translation (species RF)

Each cohort's Youden-J optimal threshold was estimated locally on its held-out predictions, observed sensitivity/specificity were measured at that threshold, and PPV / NPV / NNT were reweighted via Bayes' rule across four representative population prevalences (0.5%, 2%, 5%, 10%). Sensitivity and specificity are prevalence-invariant, so any shift in PPV across cohorts at a fixed prevalence reflects genuine site-level differences in the discrimination ceiling of the species RF.

At an assumed 5% target-population prevalence, the species RF clears the PPV > 20% clinically-meaningful triage bar in 5 of 10 cohorts: ThomasAM_2018b (n=60, PPV=0.467, NNT=2.1); WirbelJ_2018 (n=125, PPV=0.432, NNT=2.3); ZellerG_2014 (n=114, PPV=0.369, NNT=2.7); FengQ_2015 (n=107, PPV=0.253, NNT=3.9); GuptaA_2019 (n=60, PPV=0.215, NNT=4.7). These are the sites where the model could plausibly be deployed as a pre-colonoscopy enrichment layer. By contrast the weakest cohorts (ThomasAM_2018a (n=53, PPV=0.083, NNT=12.0); YachidaS_2019 (n=508, PPV=0.087, NNT=11.5); VogtmannE_2016 (n=104, PPV=0.124, NNT=8.1)) sit at or below the pooled 5%-prevalence reference of PPV = 0.114 (NNT ≈ 8.8), reflecting the same sensitivity/specificity ceiling seen in the pooled LODO analysis.
