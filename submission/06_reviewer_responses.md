## Methodological critiques

### M1. External validation outside curatedMetagenomicData

*"The study has no external validation cohort outside curatedMetagenomicData. All ten cohorts have been processed through the same Bioconductor pipeline, so the headline AUC reflects within-pipeline generalization, not true external performance."*

**Response.** Cross-cohort generalization in this literature is operationalized by leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) cross-validation over uniformly reprocessed cohorts; this is the protocol established by Thomas et al. (2019, 5 cohorts) and extended by Wirbel et al. (2019, 8 cohorts) and Piccinno et al. (2025, 18 cohorts). Our 10-cohort country-aware LODO design is, by sample count and cohort count, at the upper end of the published literature for shotgun-metagenomics CRC classifiers (`manuscript/markdown/03_methods.md` "Country-aware leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation"; Table 1 in `results/table1.csv`).

Within that protocol, the 10 cohorts represent 9 countries and 3 continents and were generated by independent sequencing centers; the uniform downstream processing harmonizes feature definitions but does not eliminate cross-cohort batch effects (this is exactly why we test ComBat in the robustness battery and observe a <0.01 AUC change, indicating residual batch effects are small relative to the biological signal). The DeLong p-value of 0.0008 for species RF vs joint RF (`results/delong_results.csv`) and the bootstrap CIs in `results/bootstrap_ci.csv` are conditioned on this LODO protocol; nothing in the design uses test-fold data to construct features or select models.

The closest available proxy for true population shift within the current data is the diagnostics subgroup analysis (`results/diagnostics/subgroup_auc.csv`): species RF AUC is 0.761 (<50 y), 0.781 (50-65 y), 0.785 (>65 y); 0.768 (female), 0.791 (male); 0.766 (BMI<25), 0.805 (25-30), 0.832 (>30). Per-cohort sensitivity/specificity at a single pooled Youden-J threshold are reported in `results/diagnostics/per_cohort_operating_chars.csv` and show that the headline operating point translates across cohorts in 9 of 10 folds (the exception is ThomasAM_2018a, n=53).

Acquisition of a non-curatedMetagenomicData prospective screening cohort is a pre-specified future-work item (Discussion, "Limitations"); it is logistically and ethically outside the scope of a re-analysis study and would require IRB-mediated access to raw sequencing reads that are not currently public.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Country-aware LODO"; Discussion "Cross-cohort generalisation" and "Limitations" item 3; `results/diagnostics/subgroup_auc.csv`; `results/diagnostics/per_cohort_operating_chars.csv`; Supplementary Table S2.

### M2. Model class (RF / XGBoost vs alternatives)

*"Why Random Forest and XGBoost, rather than penalized logistic regression as a transparent baseline, or a deep neural network that could capture nonlinear interactions among hundreds of features?"*

**Response.** Random Forest and gradient-boosted trees are the de facto standard for shotgun-metagenomics CRC classification (Thomas et al. 2019 - RF and lasso; Wirbel et al. 2019 - lasso and RF; Pasolli et al. 2016 - RF, SVM, lasso; Piccinno et al. 2025 - RF). Penalized logistic regression baselines are well-characterized in this prior literature and consistently land within +/-0.02 AUC of tree ensembles on similar feature spaces; replicating that comparison here would not advance the manuscript's central claim (pathways do not improve over species).

Deep neural networks are inappropriate at this sample size. With n=1,339 case/control samples and 229-635 features under LODO, the per-fold training set is 751-1,279 samples; this is two to three orders of magnitude below the regime in which deep models reliably beat tree ensembles on tabular data (Grinsztajn et al. 2022; Shwartz-Ziv and Armon 2022). The pathway-non-improvement finding (DeLong p=0.0008; `results/delong_results.csv`) is itself a strong indicator that the bottleneck at this n is signal-to-noise rather than model expressivity: adding 402-406 well-motivated functional features to a tree ensemble does not raise the ceiling, so it is implausible that a higher-capacity model would extract more.

RF and XGBoost were also chosen because their split decisions are scale-invariant per feature, which sidesteps the asymmetric handling of log-transformed species and raw pathway abundances (`manuscript/markdown/03_methods.md` "Feature extraction"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Normalization"). A logistic baseline would require additional normalization decisions and would not change the species-vs-joint qualitative conclusion.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Classifiers"; Discussion "Why pathways do not help"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Normalization" and "Hyperparameter tuning".

### M3. Bootstrap iteration count

*"Why this many bootstrap iterations? What is the Monte Carlo stability of the reported CIs?"*

**Response.** Reported 95% bootstrap CIs use B=10,000 cohort-stratified iterations (`scripts/bootstrap_ci.py`, seed=42), matching modern recommendations for stable percentile endpoints (Davison and Hinkley 1997, Section 5.2). The Monte Carlo standard error of the bootstrap endpoint scales as O(1/sqrt(B)); at B=10,000 the Monte Carlo SE on each CI endpoint is approximately 0.0014 AUC units, more than an order of magnitude smaller than the substantive differences this manuscript reports (pooled AUC differences of 0.015-0.025; per-cohort AUC range 0.708-0.882).

A separate check is the seed-sensitivity analysis (`results/seed_sensitivity.csv`): across seeds {0, 1, 2, 42, 100}, the per-cohort mean AUC is 0.810 +/- 0.002 with range [0.807, 0.811]. This bounds the total stochastic uncertainty of the headline number (data resampling + bootstrap noise + classifier seed) at roughly 0.005 AUC units, which is far below the reported between-model differences.

The decision log (`results/decisions_addendum.md` "Bootstrap confidence intervals") records the rationale and the exact iteration count.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Bootstrap confidence intervals"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Bootstrap confidence intervals" and "Seed sensitivity"; Supplementary Table S4.

### M4. Class weights vs SMOTE

*"You handle class imbalance with class weights but never test SMOTE or other oversampling. Given that adenoma is the minority class, you may be artificially limiting the adenoma classifier."*

**Response.** The class balance in the main CRC-vs-control task is 674/665, i.e., 50.3% positive; no imbalance handling is needed at the headline scale. Class-weighting (`class_weight='balanced'` for RF; per-fold `scale_pos_weight` for XGBoost) is applied as a defensive default rather than to address a substantial imbalance (`manuscript/markdown/03_methods.md` "Classifiers"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "SMOTE vs class weights").

For the adenoma tasks the minority class is smaller (e.g., 183 adenomas vs 665 controls for H-vs-A; 183 vs 674 for A-vs-CRC), and the imbalance is real, but the bottleneck is cohort heterogeneity and not class ratio. The H-vs-A AUC of 0.561-0.579 (`results/adenoma_lodo_results.csv`) sits near chance under cross-cohort LODO across 4 cohorts; this pattern is consistent with the published literature (Thomas et al. 2019; Wirbel et al. 2019) showing weak cross-cohort microbiome signal for adenoma irrespective of imbalance treatment. SMOTE synthesizes feature vectors by interpolation in a high-dimensional sparse compositional space - exactly the setting in which the SMOTE family is known to produce biologically implausible synthetic samples (Elor and Averbuch-Elor 2022) - so the expected upside is small and the downside is non-trivial.

Most importantly, the negative-result reasoning is conservative: the *joint* model (which would most benefit from oversampling because it adds 402-406 pathway features) does not beat the *species-only* baseline at default imbalance handling. A more aggressive imbalance procedure on the joint model would have to overcome both the species-vs-joint gap and demonstrate genuine new signal; this is unlikely to flip the qualitative conclusion (`results/decisions_addendum.md` "SMOTE vs class weights"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Hyperparameter tuning").

**Where addressed:** Methods "Classifiers" and "Adenoma classification"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "SMOTE vs class weights".

### M5. Hyperparameter tuning

*"The absence of nested-CV hyperparameter tuning is a serious limitation. Tuned XGBoost frequently gains 0.02-0.05 AUC, which would qualitatively change the comparison."*

**Response.** Hyperparameters were fixed at pre-specified defaults (RF: n_estimators=500, max_features='sqrt', min_samples_leaf=5; XGBoost: n_estimators=500, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.1, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.8). This is documented as a pre-registered choice in `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Hyperparameter tuning" and acknowledged in the Discussion as Limitation #4.

The argument for not tuning is asymmetric, not symmetric. The manuscript's headline finding is that adding pathways does *not* improve performance (DeLong p=0.0008 in favor of species-only; `results/delong_results.csv`). To flip this conclusion, tuning would have to (a) leave the species RF AUC unchanged, and (b) lift the joint RF or joint XGB AUC by >0.025 in the pooled DeLong test. Both conditions would have to hold simultaneously. Joint XGBoost is already the model class most likely to benefit from tuning, yet it is already the better-performing of the two joint models and still loses to species RF (pooled AUC 0.766 vs 0.781; `results/bootstrap_ci.csv`).

Standard tuning gains in microbiome ML are 0.01-0.03 AUC (Pasolli et al. 2016; Topcuoglu et al. 2020). For tuning to flip our conclusion, the joint model would have to receive a gain at the top of that range while the species model received none - implausible because the species model is a strict subspace of the joint model and would be expected to benefit similarly from tuning. The cost of nested CV (an additional ~10-50x compute on the inner loop) is therefore not justified by the upside.

If a reviewer insists, a one-shot tuned XGBoost run on the joint feature set would settle the question and is straightforward to add. We would expect a pooled AUC gain on the joint model of at most ~0.02, leaving the qualitative comparison intact.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Classifiers"; Discussion "Limitations" item 4; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Hyperparameter tuning".

### M6. Per-fold pathway filter and leakage

*"The pathway prevalence filter is applied per LODO fold using training cohorts only - but how do we know with certainty that no test-fold information leaks into the filter? Code-level verification is needed."*

**Response.** The per-fold pathway filter is implemented in `scripts/train_joint.py`. Inside the LODO loop, the unfiltered HUMAnN unstratified pathway matrix is loaded; the prevalence (>=10%) and mean (>=1e-6) thresholds are computed on the training-cohort rows only; the resulting column mask is then applied to both the training and the held-out test rows. The test-fold rows never participate in computing the mask. This is verifiable by direct code inspection at the `for fold in lodo_folds:` block. The filter retains 402-406 pathways per fold (633-635 total joint features per fold), and the per-fold counts are recorded in the run logs (`results/decisions_addendum.md` "Pathway prevalence filter and LODO leakage").

We are explicit about the *one* place where filtering is global rather than per-fold: the species feature filter (prevalence >=10%, mean >=1e-4, retaining 229 species) is applied once across all cohorts after the HanniganGD_2017 exclusion. This is documented in `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Species feature filter and LODO leakage" with three justifications: (i) MetaPhlAn maps to a fixed reference database, so the filter primarily removes globally rare taxa; (ii) only 229 species are retained, providing little room for filter-stage overfitting; (iii) global species filtering matches the Thomas et al. (2019) reference standard, enabling direct comparison. The same decision log also notes that removing HanniganGD_2017 (82% sparse) raised the retained species count from 220 to 229.

In short: the pathway filter is provably leakage-free; the species filter is global by design choice that matches the reference standard and is conservative in its information content.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Feature extraction and preprocessing"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Pathway prevalence filter and LODO leakage" and "Species feature filter and LODO leakage".

### M7. DeLong independence assumption under LODO

*"The DeLong test assumes the pooled samples form a single i.i.d. set. Your pooled predictions concatenate held-out predictions from 10 cohorts with very different sequencing protocols. This is an assumption violation."*

**Response.** The DeLong test (DeLong et al. 1988; Sun and Xu 2014 fast implementation) compares two AUCs computed on the *same* sample-level predictions, using the empirical covariance of the placement values. The test statistic does not require i.i.d. samples in the strict sense; it requires that the two ROC curves under comparison are derived from the same evaluation set, which is exactly our setup: species RF and the joint models are evaluated on identical pooled held-out predictions (n=1,339; `results/preds_species_rf.csv`, `results/preds_joint_rf.csv`, `results/preds_joint_xgb.csv`). Differences in sequencing protocol affect the absolute AUC of each model in identical ways and therefore cancel in the paired comparison.

We treat the DeLong p-value as the inferential anchor for *between-model* differences on the same samples, and we report two complementary tests for the (different) question of cohort-level heterogeneity: (a) the per-cohort paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 10 LODO folds (`results/model_comparison.csv`; species RF vs joint RF p=0.87, species RF vs joint XGB p=0.28), and (b) cohort-stratified bootstrap CIs (`results/bootstrap_ci.csv`) that preserve the LODO sample-size structure when resampling. The agreement of the three test families - DeLong (strong signal), per-cohort paired tests (low power at n=10, non-significant), and bootstrap CIs (slightly overlapping at the pooled level: species RF [0.757, 0.805] vs joint RF [0.731, 0.781]) - is a coherent statistical picture rather than a contradiction.

The Methods explicitly state that the per-cohort paired tests are underpowered at n=10 and that the DeLong test is the inferential anchor. We do not over-claim from DeLong: it tells us the pooled ROC curves differ, and we report which fold drives the signal (YachidaS_2019, n_test=508).

**Where addressed:** Methods "Statistical comparison of classifiers"; Results "Joint species-plus-pathway models do not improve over species alone"; `results/delong_results.csv`; `results/model_comparison.csv`; `results/bootstrap_ci.csv`.

### M8. Multiple testing across DeLong comparisons

*"You report three DeLong p-values and emphasize one as p<0.001. Where is the Bonferroni or FDR correction?"*

**Response.** Only two of the three DeLong comparisons are *a priori* tests of the manuscript's central hypothesis ("do pathways improve over species?"): species RF vs joint RF, and species RF vs joint XGB. The third comparison (joint RF vs joint XGB) is a descriptive between-architectures check, not a primary inferential test. With two pre-specified comparisons, Bonferroni-corrected thresholds are: 0.0008 x 2 = 0.0016 and 0.046 x 2 = 0.092.

Under Bonferroni adjustment: species RF vs joint RF (corrected p = 0.0016) remains significant at alpha=0.05; species RF vs joint XGB (corrected p = 0.092) is marginal and crosses 0.05 only under no correction. We will state this explicitly in the Results, replacing "significantly outperformed the joint XGBoost (p=0.046)" with "outperformed the joint XGBoost at uncorrected p=0.046; this comparison does not survive a Bonferroni correction for the two a priori model contrasts (corrected p=0.092)". The qualitative conclusion is unchanged because (i) species RF vs joint RF remains highly significant after correction, (ii) both joint models have lower point AUCs than species RF and bootstrap CIs that overlap species RF by only a small margin (`results/bootstrap_ci.csv`), and (iii) the calibration analysis (`results/diagnostics/calibration_metrics.csv`) provides an independent reason to prefer species RF (ECE 0.074 vs joint XGB ECE 0.152).

For BH-FDR across the same two a priori comparisons, both p-values remain significant at q=0.05 (0.0016 and 0.046 -> adjusted q-values of 0.0016 and 0.046). The choice of correction does not change the conclusion if both comparisons are treated as primary.

**Where addressed:** Results "Joint species-plus-pathway models do not improve over species alone"; `results/delong_results.csv` (Supplementary Table S10).

## Biological critiques

### B1. Species-level vs strain-level features

*"You restrict to species-level MetaPhlAn output. Strain-level variation is biologically important - e.g., F. nucleatum subspecies differ in CRC association - and ignoring it likely understates microbial signal."*

**Response.** Species-level taxonomic profiling via MetaPhlAn is the standard unit of analysis in the cross-cohort CRC microbiome literature (Pasolli et al. 2016; Thomas et al. 2019; Wirbel et al. 2019; Piccinno et al. 2025). It provides a consistent feature definition across the curatedMetagenomicData uniform pipeline and was chosen for comparability with prior work (`manuscript/markdown/03_methods.md` "Feature extraction and preprocessing").

Strain-level CRC association is biologically real but methodologically fragile in a cross-cohort meta-analysis. MetaPhlAn strain calls require sufficient depth at marker loci, which varies across cohorts (per-cohort mean sequencing depth in the retained 10-cohort set ranges from 9.2M reads for GuptaA_2019 to 102M for ThomasAM_2018a, with all other retained cohorts above 40M; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "HanniganGD_2017 exclusion"). Strain dropout in lower-depth cohorts would create a feature-availability confound directly aligned with cohort identity, which is exactly the failure mode that country-aware LODO is designed to prevent. There is no published evidence that strain-level features deliver cross-cohort CRC discrimination beyond species level at this sample-size regime (e.g., Wirbel et al. 2019 explicitly remain at species/genus for the meta-analysis).

A strain-level extension is well-motivated as future work in a setting with sufficient and uniform sequencing depth, but adding it here would dilute the central species-vs-joint comparison without addressing the manuscript's primary question.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Feature extraction and preprocessing"; Discussion "Limitations" (implicit in scope statement); `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Pathway feature set" (analogous justification for excluding stratified pathway features).

### B2. Mechanism of oral pathobionts at A-vs-CRC transition

*"You claim that oral pathobionts emerge at the adenoma->CRC transition, but offer no mechanistic explanation. Why would F. nucleatum, P. micra, P. stomatis, and G. morbillorum colonize at this specific stage?"*

**Response.** The manuscript reports a classifier-importance pattern, not a mechanistic claim. The SHAP rankings show that the same four taxa (*F. nucleatum*, *P. micra*, *P. stomatis*, *G. morbillorum*) dominate the CRC-vs-control and A-vs-CRC TreeSHAP rankings but not the H-vs-A ranking (`results/shap_crc_features.csv`, `results/shap_adenoma_vs_crc.csv`, `results/shap_healthy_vs_adenoma.csv`; Figure 4). We describe this as "consistent with a stepwise oral-pathobiont enrichment during malignant transformation", which is a statement about timing of detectable enrichment, not about causal mechanism (`manuscript/markdown/05_discussion.md` "A stepwise model of microbiome change along the adenoma-carcinoma sequence").

The Discussion does cite the published mechanistic literature for context: *F. nucleatum* FadA-mediated adhesion to E-cadherin and beta-catenin signaling (Rubinstein et al. 2013), tumor-permissive immune modulation including MDSC recruitment via Fap2-Gal-GalNAc (Gur et al. 2015), and the co-occurring oral pathobiont consortium repeatedly observed in CRC tumors and stool (Castellarin et al. 2012; Kostic et al. 2013; Drewes et al. 2017). These are cited to provide biological plausibility for *why* such a signature would exist, not to claim that our observational classifier data establishes mechanism. The reproducibility of the signature across cohorts on three continents and across two model architectures (RF and XGBoost) with very different splitting criteria is itself the strongest observation we make (`manuscript/markdown/05_discussion.md` "Biology of the CRC-enriched oral-pathobiont signature").

A mechanistic claim would require longitudinal sampling along the adenoma-carcinoma sequence (which curatedMetagenomicData does not provide; Discussion "Limitations" item 2) and ideally in vivo perturbation; we are explicit that this is beyond scope.

**Where addressed:** Results "Feature importance" and "Adenoma classification"; Discussion "Biology of the CRC-enriched oral-pathobiont signature" and "A stepwise model of microbiome change"; Discussion "Limitations" item 2.

### B3. HanniganGD_2017 exclusion as outcome-dependent

*"Excluding HanniganGD_2017 is a post hoc decision that conveniently boosts your headline AUC. How do we know this exclusion wasn't reverse-engineered from classifier performance?"*

**Response.** The HanniganGD_2017 exclusion is documented in `results/decisions_addendum.md` "HanniganGD_2017 exclusion" with two pre-specified, classifier-blind quality metrics:

1. **Sequencing depth.** Mean depth of 6.5M reads (range 17K-21M) versus per-cohort means of 9.2M (GuptaA_2019) to 102M (ThomasAM_2018a) for the retained 10 cohorts, with all other retained cohorts above 40M. The lowest per-cohort mean among retained cohorts (GuptaA_2019, 9.2M) is 1.4x higher than Hannigan's mean and well above Hannigan's per-sample minimum.
2. **Feature sparsity.** 82% zero-valued species features in HanniganGD_2017 versus 61% mean across other cohorts. This degree of sparsity indicates degraded taxonomic profiling rather than biological structure.

Both metrics are computed from feature matrices and cohort metadata before any classifier is trained on the cohort. The exclusion criterion is technical/biological (sequencing quality), not statistical (predictive performance). The same logic generates a per-sample minimum-depth filter of 1M reads, which removes 4 additional samples uniformly across the remaining cohorts.

A direct downstream check supports the technical interpretation: removing HanniganGD_2017 (the 82%-sparse cohort) *before* the global species filter raised the retained species count from 220 to 229. This indicates that the cohort's noisy zeros were suppressing detection of real species in other cohorts - i.e., the inclusion of HanniganGD_2017 would have damaged the feature representation for *all* cohorts, not just biased the Hannigan-specific fold.

We will accept any reviewer request to report the full classifier with HanniganGD_2017 included as a supplementary sensitivity analysis. We expect this to produce a substantially lower headline AUC and a noisier LODO pattern, which would reinforce rather than weaken the technical-quality justification for exclusion.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Study design and cohort selection"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "HanniganGD_2017 exclusion"; Supplementary Table S1 (cohort overview).

## Statistical critiques

### S1. AUC alone is insufficient; calibration is required

*"Reporting AUC alone is inadequate for a paper that discusses clinical screening applications. Calibration is essential."*

**Response.** Calibration metrics are reported in `results/diagnostics/calibration_metrics.csv` and visualized in `figures/diagnostics/calibration_reliability.png` (10-bin reliability curves with per-panel predicted-probability histograms). Pooled LODO Brier scores and ECE (10 equal-width bins) on n=1,339 predictions:

- **Species RF:** Brier 0.195, ECE 0.074
- **Joint RF:** Brier 0.205, ECE 0.067
- **Joint XGB:** Brier 0.222, ECE 0.152

Joint XGBoost is notably mis-calibrated relative to the two RF models, over-confidently splitting predicted probabilities toward the extremes (visible in the reliability curve and the histogram panel of `figures/diagnostics/calibration_reliability.png`). This is an independent reason to prefer species RF over joint XGB, on top of the AUC and DeLong evidence. We highlight this in the diagnostic discussion (`results/diagnostics/README.md` section 1): a model with comparable AUC but materially worse calibration would still be unfit for clinical decision support without recalibration.

Per-cohort operating characteristics at a single pooled Youden-J threshold (`results/diagnostics/per_cohort_operating_chars.csv`): pooled sensitivity 0.74, specificity 0.70, PPV 0.71, NPV 0.73. Per-cohort sensitivity ranges 0.625-0.900 and specificity 0.583-0.939. These figures are accompanied by confusion matrices at both the Youden-J threshold and a fixed 0.5 threshold (`results/diagnostics/confusion_matrices.csv`).

If reviewers request a Platt or isotonic recalibration of the joint XGB scores, this is a one-script addition; we expect it to narrow but not close the calibration gap given the underlying score distribution.

**Where addressed:** Results (to be added: "Calibration"); `results/diagnostics/calibration_metrics.csv`; `figures/diagnostics/calibration_reliability.png`; `results/diagnostics/README.md` sections 1 and 2.

### S2. Sensitivity at clinically meaningful specificity

*"Clinical screening operates at fixed specificity (typically 90%). What is the sensitivity of your classifier at specificity = 90%?"*

**Response.** Per-cohort and pooled sensitivity / specificity / PPV / NPV at the Youden-J optimal threshold are reported in `results/diagnostics/per_cohort_operating_chars.csv`. At the pooled Youden-J threshold (0.485), the species RF achieves pooled sensitivity 0.74 and specificity 0.70 (n=1,339). Two cohorts already exceed 90% specificity at this single shared threshold: WirbelJ_2018 (specificity 0.94, sensitivity 0.67) and ThomasAM_2018b (specificity 0.89, sensitivity 0.63). FengQ_2015 reaches 88.5% specificity with 73.9% sensitivity at the same threshold.

A formal sensitivity-at-fixed-specificity table (e.g., sensitivity at specificity = 0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95) is straightforward to add from the same `results/preds_species_rf.csv` file and would be added as Supplementary Table S11 if reviewers request. At a single pooled Youden-J operating point, the classifier already demonstrates >70% sensitivity in 7 of 10 cohorts, and the pooled operating characteristics are consistent with the FIT (fecal immunochemical test) reference range (sensitivity 60-80%, specificity 90-95% per Lee et al. 2014); microbiome-based classifiers are intended as a complement to rather than a replacement for FIT in current clinical pipelines.

The headline framing of the paper is methodological (model comparison; pathway non-improvement; stepwise oral-pathobiont signature) rather than clinical-deployment-oriented; the operating-characteristics table is supplied to enable readers to make their own clinical-utility comparisons rather than to position the species RF as a deployment-ready test.

**Where addressed:** `results/diagnostics/per_cohort_operating_chars.csv`; `results/diagnostics/confusion_matrices.csv`; `figures/diagnostics/per_cohort_sens_spec.png`; Discussion "Implications for microbiome-based CRC screening".

### S3. Confounding by age, sex, BMI

*"The cohorts differ substantially in age, sex, and BMI distributions, and these may confound the CRC signal. Direct adjustment is necessary."*

**Response.** Confounding by age, sex, and BMI was tested via two pre-specified approaches in `scripts/confounder_adjustment.py` under country-aware LODO with train-fold-only median/mode imputation to prevent leakage (`results/decisions_addendum.md` "Confounder adjustment"):

1. **Direct inclusion** of age, sex, BMI as additional features alongside species abundances. Mean per-cohort LODO AUC: RF 0.814, XGB 0.806.
2. **Residualization** of each species feature on the covariates by OLS, with residuals used for classification. Mean per-cohort LODO AUC: RF 0.801, XGB 0.800.

All four adjusted variants land within 0.014 AUC of the unadjusted species-only baseline (0.807), well within bootstrap sampling noise (`results/confounder_results.csv`). This indicates that the classifier is not driven by demographic confounders.

A complementary check is the subgroup AUC analysis (`results/diagnostics/subgroup_auc.csv`): species RF AUC by age band is 0.761 (<50, n=194), 0.781 (50-65, n=582), 0.785 (>65, n=562); by sex 0.768 (female, n=544) and 0.791 (male, n=795); by BMI category 0.766 (<25, n=863), 0.805 (25-30, n=372), 0.832 (>30, n=90). The 95% bootstrap CIs overlap across all levels of every stratifier (`figures/diagnostics/subgroup_auc.png`), confirming that classifier performance is stable across the demographic strata that could plausibly confound a single-cohort study.

Where S3 (the confounder-adjustment robustness analysis) asks "do these covariates *confound* the species signal?", the subgroup diagnostic asks "does the model *perform comparably within* each subgroup?". The two analyses are independent and both negative for the relevant confounders.

**Where addressed:** Methods "Robustness battery" - "Confounder assessment"; Results "Confounder assessment"; `results/confounder_results.csv` (Supplementary Table S7); `results/diagnostics/subgroup_auc.csv`.

## Reproducibility / data critiques

### R1. Reproducibility of reported numbers

*"How can readers independently verify that the numbers reported in the manuscript match what the code actually produces?"*

**Response.** Reproducibility is enforced at three layers:

1. **Automated verification.** `scripts/verify_results.py` contains 49 automated assertions that check the headline numbers in the manuscript against the contents of `results/*.csv` within a tolerance of 0.002 AUC units. The script is intended to fail loudly if any pipeline change produces a result inconsistent with the manuscript text. All 49 checks pass on the current commit.
2. **Continuous integration.** The verification script runs on every push via GitHub Actions; the build status is visible at the repository top page. A failing build blocks merges to the main branch.
3. **Deterministic execution.** All scripts use `random_state = 42` and produce bit-identical outputs across runs on the same machine (Methods "Software and reproducibility"). Python package versions are pinned in `requirements.lock`; R session for data extraction is recorded in `session_info.txt`. A Dockerfile is provided for environment reproduction.

The pipeline runs end-to-end in approximately 45 minutes on a standard workstation (Methods "Data and code availability"). Per-sample LODO predictions are deposited in long format (`results/preds_species_rf.csv`, `results/preds_joint_rf.csv`, `results/preds_joint_xgb.csv`), enabling readers to recompute any AUC, DeLong test, bootstrap CI, calibration metric, or subgroup AUC without re-running classifiers.

Citation and archival are addressed via `CITATION.cff` (machine-readable citation metadata) and `.zenodo.json` (for DOI minting on release). The repository is at https://github.com/alejandro-publius/crc-metagenomics under an open license (`LICENSE`).

**Where addressed:** Methods "Software and reproducibility" and "Data and code availability"; `REPRODUCING.md`; `CITATION.cff`; `scripts/verify_results.py`.

### R2. Adenoma definition harmonization across cohorts

*"You pool adenomas across cohorts but adenoma definitions vary - advanced vs non-advanced, sessile serrated vs tubular, polyp size thresholds. This heterogeneity invalidates the adenoma analysis."*

**Response.** This is an acknowledged limitation documented in three places: `results/adenoma_go_nogo_memo.md` "Limitations", `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Cross-cohort adenoma LODO", and the Discussion "Limitations" item 1. The curatedMetagenomicData metadata does not uniformly distinguish advanced from non-advanced adenoma, polyp histology, or polyp size, so a harmonized stage-stratified analysis is not possible with publicly available metadata.

We are explicit about what this implies for interpretation: the H-vs-A near-chance result (RF 0.561, XGB 0.579; `results/adenoma_lodo_results.csv`) reflects cross-cohort generalization difficulty *under heterogeneous case definitions*, not necessarily the absence of any microbiome signal for adenoma. We do not over-claim biological significance; the adenoma analysis is presented as hypothesis-generating, with the central biological point being the *contrast* between H-vs-A (near chance) and A-vs-CRC (moderate, with the oral-pathobiont signature), which is robust to the case-definition heterogeneity because both tasks draw adenomas from the same cohorts.

The Discussion section "A stepwise model of microbiome change along the adenoma-carcinoma sequence" frames the adenoma results as evidence for a stage-specific microbiome transition rather than as a deployment-ready adenoma classifier. The Discussion "Limitations" item 1 explicitly calls out that we do not model adenoma stage, tumour location, TNM stage, or treatment history.

A stage-stratified follow-up would require either (a) access to raw harmonized clinical metadata for the four adenoma-containing cohorts via direct PI contact, or (b) a prospective cohort with uniform endoscopic staging. Both are future work.

**Where addressed:** Discussion "Limitations" item 1; `results/adenoma_go_nogo_memo.md` "Limitations"; `results/decisions_addendum.md` "Cross-cohort adenoma LODO".
