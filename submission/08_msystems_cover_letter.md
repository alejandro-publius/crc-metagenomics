Dear Editors of mSystems,

We submit "Biological detail does not guarantee portability of colorectal cancer metagenomic classifiers" for consideration as a Research Article.

Microbiome prediction studies often assume that more detailed genes, pathways, or mechanisms will generalize better than taxonomic features. We test that assumption prospectively across ten development cohorts and an untouched 200-sample external cohort. Under country-aware leave-one-dataset-out validation, gene families, a checksum-frozen mechanism panel, and source-only correction of species-resolved pathways did not consistently improve on a species reference. A label-free model intended to warn about target-cohort failure was also less accurate than a simple historical-performance comparator. Nevertheless, the frozen species model retained discrimination in the external cohort (AUC 0.798; 95% CI 0.737-0.855).

We believe this work fits mSystems because it integrates taxonomic, functional, gene-family, and mechanism-level measurements to answer a systems-level question about the transport of microbial community signals. The contribution is not a new biomarker list. It is an auditable stress test showing that biological specificity and measurable dataset shift do not automatically translate into better cross-study performance. The external sample set, mapping rule, model, and stop rule were committed before scoring, and positive and negative findings are reported together.

All source data are public. Code, derived tables, per-sample predictions, bootstrap replicates, pinned environments, and decision records are available in the linked repository; a permanent archival DOI will be inserted before submission.
