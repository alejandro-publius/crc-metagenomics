# Post-Preprint Journal Targets (Ranked)

Once the bioRxiv DOI is live, submit to a journal. The list below is
ordered by realistic acceptance probability for *this specific paper*
(10-cohort meta-analysis, negative-result framing on pathway features,
solo undergraduate first author + biologist co-author, no big-lab brand).
Probability estimates are rough planning numbers, not guarantees.

## 1. Microbiome (BMC) — best fit, ~30-40%
What they want: methodologically rigorous microbiome studies with
clear biological interpretation, especially meta-analyses and
cross-cohort generalization work. Pathway negative-result framing
fits their stated openness to "robust null findings". IF ~14.
Watch-outs: editors expect very clean reproducibility (we have it),
and a strong biological discussion (we have the oral-pathobiont story).

## 2. mSystems (ASM) — fastest, ~50-60%
What they want: systems-level microbiome biology with computational
emphasis. Friendly to meta-analyses, methods comparisons, and
honest negative results. Faster review (median ~6-8 weeks),
open access. IF ~7. Best choice if the priority is "get it published
this year" rather than impact factor.

## 3. Genome Medicine (BMC) — ~25-35%
What they want: clinically translatable genomic / multi-omic studies.
The CRC screening framing and species-only parsimony argument
translate well into a clinical-utility story. IF ~12. Higher bar
than mSystems; editors may push back on the dataset being entirely
public re-analysis without a held-out clinical validation cohort.

## 4. Gut Microbes (Taylor & Francis) — ~30%
What they want: gut-microbiome papers with clear disease relevance,
including CRC. Comfortable home for the topic; somewhat less focused
on rigorous methods comparison than Microbiome / mSystems. IF ~12.
Reasonable second-choice after Microbiome.

## 5. eLife — ~20-30%
What they want: novel, methodologically sound work with broad
interest; open review and assessment model now in place. The
negative-result-on-pathways finding plays well with eLife's
appetite for "important but not flashy" papers. IF ~7. Risk:
the new eLife model means even accepted papers can carry a mixed
public assessment, which the authors should be ready for.

---

Recommended path: submit to **Microbiome** first; on rejection, send
to **mSystems** (fastest re-route). Keep Genome Medicine / Gut
Microbes / eLife in reserve.

For each target journal, tailor `submission/00_cover_letter.md` to that
journal's editor and reviewer expectations before pressing Submit.
